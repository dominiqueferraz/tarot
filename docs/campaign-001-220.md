# The atlas of the Major Arcana · levels 001–220

**Volume split.** The Major Arcana now span three volumes, one per act: the Fool's prologue and I–VII, then VIII–XIV, then XV–XXI. Chapters lengthen to about 20, 24 and 28 boards (see [design notes](design-notes.md#design-space)). The ten-slot chapters below are the first movement of each chapter. Level numbers will be reassigned when chapters are re-authored.

**Production status.** Tarot Squared implements the first ten deals with shared square and rectangle rules. The Mooring introduces linked targets, The Ferryman’s Fare introduces Coin deposits, Market Day combines three scoring suits at a far-bank bread stall, The Fair Split introduces targets that must finish together, and From Behind introduces a lamp with one accepted aim direction. Keep Me a Partner fills the fishing crew's basin, and Night Fishing lights their departure and frees their boat. The archived web playtest contains five earlier quota deals and three separate Wreath studies for lozenges, target links and teaching. All eight have recorded clears at their current seeds. The remaining numbered entries are briefs for authoring. Fool 013 has a legacy layout with two intended uses of Leap and a reproduced cardless clear under the archived web rules. Chapters I–XXI retain their legacy numbers and need layouts, ribbons and budgets. Refer to a deal by chapter and slot until renumbering. [Open the archived playtest](opening-deals.md#replaying-the-archived-prototypes) · [Opening deals](opening-deals.md)

**How to read chapters I–XXI.** The Fool follows the prologue sequence below. In the later chapters, slots 1–5 use suits alone unless a guest card is expressly named. Slot 6 lends and awards the chapter card. Slots 7–10 deal that card, plus any named guest. Ownership is permanent. Charges reset per attempt as described in the [PRD](product-requirements.md). A card is available whenever dealt; a level only requires its use if the board design says so. Slots 8 and 9 are optional. N, H and SH are proposed Normal, Hard and Super Hard tiers. Every board refills from authored ribbons. A certificate must hold over every ribbon, and where it depends on a suit being absent it names the ribbons that lack it. Each board fixes its own footprint, from 4 × 4 up to 8 × 9 (see the PRD's *Board sizes*), and its supply ribbons: open, measured, or somewhere between. Teach with a small number of fixtures and leave other groups available to trace. Every effect in the upper illustration corresponds to a cell, edge or fixture event. For object goals, give the same deal enough visible resources for different approaches. Author the decision, play forward from likely choices and verify the examples against the same supply and move limit. Every board that requires its card carries a **certificate**: a one-line reason it cannot be completed without the card, its type (Isolation, Count, Arithmetic, Reach, Timing or Budget), and whether it holds at any length or only within the budget. The slot-6 rows below state the certificate each layout must guarantee.

**Move limits.** Every board shows a move limit. Play continues until the goal is complete, the moves run out or the player returns to the path. Running out of moves or returning to the path from an unfinished deal costs one life from the shared pool of five. Retry is available while a life remains. Each spent life returns 20 minutes after it was spent.
- **What counts.** Count paid strokes. Card activation is free.
- **Par** is an optional efficiency target achieved on the authored deal. Set the clear limit to accommodate its different approaches.
- **Budget.** Use the [PRD's authoring defaults](product-requirements.md#challenge-hints-and-fairness). Each level and each encounter phase with its own limit starts with at least 10 moves. Name and playtest each proposed 10-move training level or particularly demanding Hard or Super Hard challenge before accepting its limit.
- **Vary the dial.** Every board refills. Open supply carries rhythm and rest. Measured supply carries teaching moments, first readings and most Hard boards.
- **Stars.** One star for a clear, two within par plus half the slack, three for par, and a distinct mark under par. The main route never requires more than one star.
- **No spare-move puzzles.** If removing a spare move is the only interesting change from another board, redesign the layout.
- **Actors.** A moving actor can impose a tighter local deadline than the move limit.
- **Every board can be lost.** Every board has a plausible losing line, including 001, rest boards and generous open boards. Refill usually allows recovery, so a losing line either costs more moves to recover than remain, or strands something no ribbon replaces, such as a suit the ribbons lack or a piece in a dry column. Plan each chapter's first *likely* failure on purpose, and make sure every loss can be explained from visible information.

**Board brief.** Each entry should eventually state its **payoff** and its **losing line**. The payoff is the stroke that does two jobs or completes a visible chain, the board's moment in the [delight loop](product-requirements.md). Name the different ways a player can progress, one recoverable weak choice and the trade each approach makes. The individual examples below are prompts for authoring. Alongside them, state the misbelief the board overturns, its cast and its pressure (see [depth and progression](design-notes.md#depth-and-progression)).

**Route through the world.** The Fool follows a lit trail down to a river. A ferry, a roadside map and a repaired bridge open the route to the harbour. When the water rises and the seawall breaks, the Fool returns to the places and people along that route to get the harbour working again. [Campaign story map](#campaign-story-map)

## 0 · The Fool · prologue 001–020

This prologue replaces the earlier ten-board Fool chapter. Later chapters keep their old numbers until they are renumbered.

Trace a single suit through horizontal or vertical neighbours and aim with the last step, or close a square or rectangle with matching ordinary corners to clear an area. Both strokes resolve on release and refill the board. The goal tracker shows the scoring suits for each target. A later puzzle group introduces lozenges with its own combos. [Shared gesture rules](product-requirements.md#what-happens-under-the-finger) · [The Fool’s Step replication spec](opening-deals.md#replicate-the-deal)

**What it teaches.** One basic target type per suit:
- Wands light the lamp.
- Cups pour oil into the quay lamp or fill an inlet and raise its float.
- Swords cut a cord.
- Coins load a pan.
- The later falling-Coin lesson introduces a loose Coin that rings a bell when its support clears. The first five deals teach target actions without a bell objective.

It also teaches gaps and Leap. Shelves, carts, rails and runaway Coins wait for their own chapters.

**Shape and pacing.**
- **Supply.** Every board refills. Open and measured supply alternate, so each new tool first gets an open board to settle in, then a measured board that asks for an exact use of it. The prologue leans toward measured supply because it teaches the exact tricks the rest of the campaign builds on.
- **Board sizes.** 001 starts at 6 × 6, 002 narrows to 5 × 6, 003 stands tall at 6 × 7, 004 opens out to 7 × 6 and 005 expands to 8 × 8.
- **Tiers.** 008 teaches the directional lamp at Normal. Its opening scores after one setup stroke. 012 is the first remaining Hard candidate on the main route.
- **The designed first failure** is 012.
- **Fragments.** The first clear of each phase from 001 through 010 awards one fragment of the Fool’s journey. Show that fragment in the win dialog. Clearing 010 completes the journey illustration. The first reading on 013 awards the abstract Fool card and Leap.

**Tableau rule.** A Wand can support a loose Coin above a pan. In 011 a gap first interrupts a delivery; in 012 it strands a partner; in 013 it interrupts the trace itself.

**Card.** Leap lets the next trace jump one intervening cell in a straight line to a matching piece. The jumped cell is untouched, whatever it holds.

**How to read the moves column.** Where both values are set, read them as *par / limit*. A single number is the limit. Draft limits are starting points for authoring. Set par and confirm the limit by replaying different approaches on the authored deal.

## I · The Magician · 011–020

**Tableau rule.** A palette tile prints its suit onto the next ordinary piece that lands there. A narrow colored inset shows the result before release.

**Card.** Transmute changes one ordinary piece in place to a chosen suit. Fixtures and relics cannot change.
